Battery Management System (BMS)

UBC Bionics | GRASP Prosthetic Arm

Biomedical Engineering Student | Medical Devices & Bionic Prosthesis Enthusiast

Background & Overview

UBC Bionics is an undergraduate design team focused on developing innovative solutions to enhance or replace human physiological functions. The team is working on GRASP, a myoelectric prosthetic arm, and NERV, a brain-computer interface. The BMS for GRASP protects the battery from faults, optimizes health, and safeguards electronics.

The BMS was my first project with UBC Bionics. I gained hands-on experience with soldering, component selection, and PCB design in Altium. Special thanks to Justin The for research and partnership.

1.0 BMS Summary

A BMS manages battery cells, ensuring safety and reliability. Key features include:

  • Cell balancing
  • Overcurrent protection
  • Temperature sensing
  • Overcharge protection
  • Overdischarge protection

1.1 Initial Iteration

The system monitors pack voltage, temperature, and current, disconnects charging on faults, and communicates with controllers. Outputs include SOC, SOH, and fault reports.

3.0 Initial Iteration

Version 1 uses a Master-Slave topology. The master board (STM32, buck converters) is in the forearm; the slave board (protection, balancing, fuel gauging) is in the shoulder. I worked on the slave board.

Battery Protection (BQ77915)

The BQ77915 IC provides voltage, current, temperature protections and cell balancing. It protects against overvoltage, undervoltage, overcurrent, short circuit, and temperature faults.

Fuel Gauging (BQ34110)

The BQ34110 gas gauge tracks SOC and SOH, communicating via I2C. It uses CEDV technology for accurate capacity prediction and monitors current via a shunt resistor.

Technical Specifications

Battery protection (/cell)

  • OV threshold (mV): 4200; Hysteresis (mV): 200
  • UV threshold (mV): 2900; Hysteresis (mV): 400
  • OW threshold (nA): 100
  • OCD threshold (mV): 70
  • SCD threshold (mV): 120
  • OCC threshold (mV): 70
  • OTC temperature (degC): 45; OTD temperature (degC): 65; UTD temperature (degC): -10; UTC temperature (degC): 0
  • Cell balancing V_start (V): 3.8

General

  • number of series cells: 4
  • V_in (max): 25 V
  • Overvoltage threshold: 4.2V
  • Undervoltage (overdischarge) threshold: 2.9V

3.1 Lessons Learned

During testing, I realized I had mistakenly shorted the CHG and DSG MOSFETs through ground planes. To resolve this, I redesigned the system into two separate PCBs and had multiple peer reviews before fabricating. If time permitted, I would re-merge the circuits into one board, incorporate a programmable IC for configurable thresholds, and ideally integrate protection and fuel gauging into one IC to reduce footprint.
